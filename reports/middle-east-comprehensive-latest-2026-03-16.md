## 5. Shipping / Strait of Hormuz Status

### 5.1 Background (Confirmed Fact)
- The **Strait of Hormuz** is ~33 km wide at its narrowest navigable point (two-lane inbound/outbound shipping lanes of ~3 km each).
- Approximately **20–21% of global petroleum liquids** transited Hormuz in recent years (EIA data).
- It is the **only sea route** out of the Persian Gulf for oil from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, and Iran — roughly 17–20 million barrels per day.
- **Source:** Encyclopaedia Britannica (Kharg Island entry), US EIA data. [See Section 10]

### 5.2 Current Status — [REPORTED — elevated risk; full closure unconfirmed]
- **Confirmed:** Elevated maritime risk in and around the Strait; war-risk insurance premiums have risen sharply.
- **Confirmed (project44 data):** A **360% surge in ocean freight diversions** has been reported in connection with Hormuz-area disruption — vessels rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope rather than transiting the Gulf. [See Section 10]
- **Military.com reporting:** Iran has escalated attacks on ships in/near the Strait. [See Section 10]
- **Not confirmed:** A formal, declared closure of the Strait by Iran. Iran has the capacity to make transit dangerous without declaring closure — and has historically preferred this approach.
- **UKMTO/maritime advisory status:** ⚠️ For current UKMTO (UK Maritime Trade Operations) bulletins, which are the authoritative real-time source for shipping risk, check **www.ukmto.org** directly. This report cannot provide real-time advisory status.

### 5.3 What a "Practical Closure" Looks Like (Without Formal Declaration)
Iran does not need to formally declare Hormuz closed to disrupt traffic:
- **Mining threats or actual mining:** Creates extreme insurance/routing risk.
- **Drone/missile harassment of tankers:** Even one successful strike on a VLCC creates a chilling effect.
- **GPS jamming/spoofing:** Disrupts navigation in narrow waters.
- **IRGC Navy boardings/seizures:** Creates legal/insurance complications.
All of the above have been observed in prior escalation cycles. Their combination effectively prices many vessels and insurers out of the route.

## 6. Energy (Oil / LNG) Implications

### 6.1 Oil — [REPORTED — directional; live prices not available in this report]
- **Risk premium:** A meaningful geopolitical risk premium is priced into crude oil (Brent/WTI) — estimates vary but it is likely in the range of several dollars per barrel above pre-escalation baseline. **Exact figure requires a live market feed.**
- **Supply disruption scenario:** If Hormuz traffic is materially disrupted:
  - Short-term: Price spike. The IEA Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) release mechanism exists but is finite.
  - Medium-term: Global recession risk if sustained, as the global economy is deeply oil-dependent.
- **OPEC+ response:** If price spikes, OPEC+ (led by Saudi Arabia and Russia) could increase production — but Saudi Arabia itself exports through Hormuz, so it has both the incentive and the constraint.

### 6.2 LNG — [REPORTED — high risk for Qatar-dependent buyers]
- **Qatar** exports a large share of global LNG through the Strait of Hormuz.
- **Asian LNG buyers** (Japan, South Korea, China, India) would face supply risk and price spikes if Hormuz transit is severely disrupted.
- **India specifically:** India imports a meaningful share of LNG from Qatar (RasGas/QatarEnergy long-term contracts). Disruption would hit gas-based power plants and industrial consumers.
- **Verification status:** Qatar LNG routing through Hormuz is an established fact. Disruption impact analysis is forward-looking (scenario-based).

### 6.3 Refined Products / Shipping Costs
- **Freight rates:** The ~360% surge in ocean freight diversions (project44) implies sharply higher shipping costs globally — this is a supply-chain inflation input.
- **Refinery margins:** Input cost volatility (crude + freight) creates margin uncertainty for refiners like IOC.

## 8. India Lens

### 8.1 USD / INR
- **Directional signal:** INR is under pressure in a risk-off / crude-up environment.
  - India is a large net crude importer (~85% of crude needs imported).
  - Higher oil → wider current account deficit → INR pressure.
  - Global risk-off → FII outflows → additional INR pressure.
- **RBI response optionality:** The Reserve Bank of India can intervene to stabilize INR from forex reserves, but sustained intervention has limits.
- **Key level to watch:** USD/INR — if it breaks above key resistance (check live data), it signals market is pricing in significant stress.

### 8.2 Indian OMCs (IOC, BPCL, HPCL)
- **IOC (Indian Oil Corporation)** is the largest and most exposed.
  - **Negative factors:** Rising crude cost; potential under-recovery if pump prices are not raised; higher freight/insurance on crude imports; INR weakness increases import bill in local-currency terms.
  - **Positive factors (limited in acute crisis):** If prices are raised and crude stabilizes, marketing margins can recover.
  - **Policy risk:** Government may limit retail price hikes for political reasons → squeezes OMC margins.
- **Directional bias for Monday open (2026-03-16):** Bearish / under pressure — particularly if Brent is elevated and INR is weak on Sunday/early Monday Asia session.

### 8.3 Indian Airlines
- **Airlines** are highly sensitive to jet fuel (ATF) prices, which are derived from crude.
- Higher crude → higher ATF → compressed margins (especially if airlines can't pass through quickly via fare increases).
- IndiGo and Air India are the main listed/relevant names.
- **Directional bias:** Bearish in elevated-crude environment.

### 8.4 Indian Defense (HAL)
- **HAL (Hindustan Aeronautics Limited)**:
  - **Narrative tailwind:** Geopolitical tension reinforces the "defense spending" narrative — governments globally increase defense procurement.
  - **Practical caution:** HAL's order pipeline and execution are domestic-budget-driven; global war headlines don't directly accelerate Indian defense orders in the short term.
  - **Risk in acute sell-off:** Even defensive / defense names can be caught in broad risk-off deleveraging (liquid profitable positions get sold to raise cash).
- **Directional bias:** Relatively more resilient than IOC/airlines, but not immune to broad market sell-off.
